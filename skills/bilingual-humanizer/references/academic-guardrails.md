# Academic integrity and register guardrails

Read this file before revising scholarly prose in either language.

## Contents

- Preserve the argument
- Preserve evidence and citations
- Preserve method and uncertainty
- Preserve terminology across languages
- Revise by genre
- Final integrity check

## Preserve the argument

- Distinguish the research problem, claim, warrant, evidence, counterpoint, limitation, and contribution before rewriting.
- Preserve logical direction. Do not turn association into causation, interpretation into observation, possibility into fact, or a local finding into a universal claim.
- Do not add novelty claims such as “first”, “unique”, or “unprecedented” unless the source states and supports them.
- Keep analytical density when it is earned. Humanizing is not simplification, popularization, or automatic shortening.
- Do not replace a precise theoretical claim with a generic statement about identity, power, society, culture, or significance.
- Do not add an interpretive bridge merely because it is plausible. If the source moves directly from evidence to a claim, preserve that boundary or flag the gap instead of silently filling it.

## Preserve evidence and citations

- Keep every citation attached to the claim it supports. Do not move a citation across sentences if that changes its scope.
- Preserve the citation's exact claim scope while allowing the supported proposition to be restated in less formulaic language.
- Preserve author names, years, page numbers, citation keys, footnotes, quotation marks, ellipses, and bracketed additions.
- Never edit words inside a verbatim quotation or participant excerpt. If a quotation is awkward, improve the lead-in and interpretation.
- Do not merge two cited claims when the resulting sentence makes both sources appear to support the same proposition.
- Do not create a citation, reference, source consensus, or empirical detail to make prose sound more concrete.

## Preserve method and uncertainty

- Retain terms that mark evidence strength: `may`, `might`, `suggests`, `indicates`, `appears`, `可能`, `或许`, `表明`, `在一定程度上`, when methodologically justified.
- Remove only stacked or redundant hedges. If unsure whether a hedge is redundant, keep it.
- Preserve distinctions among participant account, researcher interpretation, analytic theme, and theoretical inference.
- In qualitative research, protect idiographic detail, deviant cases, researcher reflexivity, and the distance between quotation and interpretation.
- In quantitative writing, protect sample scope, estimates, uncertainty, model conditions, effect direction, and statistical notation.

## Preserve terminology across languages

- Build an internal term map for repeated Chinese and English concepts. Use one preferred rendering unless the field requires alternatives.
- Keep established abbreviations and define them only where the source does.
- Do not translate proper names, scales, constructs, methods, journal titles, or participant labels inconsistently.
- When asked to translate and humanize in one task, translate for conceptual equivalence first, verify the term map, then run the target-language style pass.

## Revise by genre

- **Literature review:** synthesize by debate, problem, mechanism, method, or gap; keep attribution visible and avoid author-by-author catalogues.
- **Theory:** make each conceptual move explicit; remove only ceremonial depth, not necessary abstraction.
- **Methods:** favor reproducibility and accurate sequence over stylistic variety. Preserve standardized method names and procedural terms.
- **Findings/analysis:** maintain the boundary between evidence and interpretation. Retain participant identifiers and quotation wording.
- **Discussion:** connect findings to theory without inflating generalizability. Preserve rival explanations and limitations.
- **Abstract:** protect objective, method, sample, result, and contribution; do not add claims to improve narrative flow.
- **Reviewer response:** remain precise and professional; do not make concessions or promises beyond the source.

## Final integrity check

Compare source and revision sentence by sentence and verify:

1. the same claims and relationships remain;
2. no fact, citation, quotation, example, or inference was added;
3. no necessary qualification disappeared;
4. evidence still supports the nearest claim;
5. key terms remain stable within and across languages;
6. revisions improve prose without rewriting the author's scholarly position.
