# Conceptual and qualitative figures

Identify whether each node represents a participant term, empirical theme, analytical construct, or theoretical proposition. Preserve the distinction in labels or captions when it affects interpretation. A proposed model must be labeled as proposed; do not present it as a tested model.

Specify the meaning of each edge: temporal order, causal hypothesis, observed association, conceptual relation, nesting, or interpretive movement. Do not use directional arrows as decoration or imply a fixed developmental sequence without temporal evidence. A causal hypothesis is not a causally identified effect. Use explicit edge labels, line styles, or a caption legend where relations differ.

Do not translate theme frequency into prevalence or significance unless the study actually supports that calculation. Do not invent numeric weights for qualitative relationships. Preserve divergent cases and scope limits; do not smooth them away to create symmetry.

Use editable SVG/PDF, native presentation shapes, or Mermaid when suitable. Confirm that final rendering preserves Chinese characters, italics, arrows, reading order, and concept labels. Supply a caption explaining evidence basis and analytical status; accessible descriptions should express the relation rather than merely list boxes.
