# Claim verification

For each material claim, track manuscript location and proposition, exact source identity, access state, relevant passage/locator, and verdict: supported, partially supported, unsupported, contradicted, or not assessable. Not assessable is not evidence that a claim is false.

Check population/context, construct, method, direction, causal versus associative language, qualification, and generalization scope. An abstract supports only what it actually states. A review reporting another author's result is a secondary source; do not pretend the original was read. Search official or publisher sources for corrections/retractions when the claim warrants it, without uploading private manuscript text.

Separate bibliographic repairs from substantive claim changes. Suggest narrowing or qualifying an overextended claim; do not attach a convenient but unread replacement reference. Mark precise quotations and page numbers as unverified until checked against the relevant edition.
