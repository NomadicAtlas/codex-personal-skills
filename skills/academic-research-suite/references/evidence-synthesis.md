# Evidence Search and Literature Synthesis

Use this workflow for literature reviews, systematic reviews, research landscapes, source verification, and claim checking.

## Search design

Define the question, date range, languages, disciplines, source types, databases or websites, and inclusion/exclusion logic before broad searching. Develop concept blocks with synonyms, translated terms, historical spellings, and adjacent disciplinary vocabulary.

Search iteratively:

1. locate anchor works and recent reviews;
2. expand through backward and forward citation chaining;
3. search missing concepts, populations, cases, and dissenting positions;
4. stop when new searches mainly repeat established positions or when the agreed scope is covered.

For systematic or meta-analytic work, record query strings, databases, dates, screening stages, exclusion reasons, duplicates, and appraisal criteria. Do not label an ordinary narrative search “systematic.”

## Evidence ledger

Track, where relevant, the full bibliographic identity and stable locator; study context, sample/corpus, method, and data; central claim and evidence actually offered; limitations, conflicts, retractions, or correction status; whether the full text was read or only metadata/abstract was available; and which manuscript claim the source can support.

Never infer that a source supports a claim from title similarity alone. Distinguish bibliographic existence from evidentiary support.

## Synthesis

Organize literature around debates, mechanisms, concepts, methods, historical shifts, or contradictions—not as a sequence of author summaries. Compare convergences and disagreements, then explain why they arise: definitions, cases, scales, measures, epistemologies, periods, or institutional contexts.

Use a matrix when several exact comparisons are needed:

| Source cluster | Question/claim | Evidence and method | Agreement/tension | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Integrity rules

- Verify quotations against the source and preserve wording and page information accurately.
- Mark inaccessible full texts and do not imply they were read.
- Search for retractions or corrections when the claim is important or contested.
- Preserve null findings and credible counterevidence.
- Do not fabricate references to make prose look complete.

## Deliverable

Provide the search scope, thematic or argumentative synthesis, major debates, evidence quality, blind spots, and a clear account of how the proposed study enters the conversation.
