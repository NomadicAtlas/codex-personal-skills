# Theory and contribution

Identify the empirical puzzle and the exact scholarly conversation before adding concepts. For a central concept, specify its definition, source when needed, analytical role, observable or interpretive evidence, competing explanation, and boundary conditions. Prefer fewer concepts doing clear work.

Trace the move from material to interpretation to conceptual claim. Distinguish a participant's vocabulary, an analytical theme, and a theoretical construct. Label new conceptual proposals as proposals; do not retroactively attribute them to participants or the original design.

Test whether theory changes the reading of concrete evidence. Remove decorative citations and generic statements about identity, power, or society that do not explain a relationship in the material. Do not manufacture an intermediate mechanism to connect an attractive theory to a quotation.

State contribution as a demonstrated change in understanding: refining a concept, qualifying an explanation, exposing a boundary, explaining a relationship, or reorganizing a debate. A new setting, topic, label, or three-part framework alone does not establish contribution. Novelty and absence claims require literature verification; do not declare a gap from an incomplete search.

Keep interpretive propositions distinct from causal mechanisms and causal identification. A temporal narrative does not alone prove causation. Describe rival readings and what evidence could discriminate among them. Preserve warranted abstraction and analytical density; specificity does not require reducing theory to everyday language.
