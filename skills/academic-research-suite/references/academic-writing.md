# Academic Planning, Drafting, and Revision

Use this workflow after the research question and evidence base are sufficiently defined.

## Argument architecture

Before drafting, specify the central answer or thesis; two to five supporting claims; the evidence required for each claim; the inferential link from evidence to claim; competing explanations or readings; and scope conditions and limitations.

Build sections around argumentative work rather than topic labels. Each section should have a function, a principal claim, supporting material, and a transition that advances the paper.

## Drafting

Write in the user's requested language and disciplinary register. Preserve analytical distinctions and causal or interpretive modesty. Integrate citations at the level of the claim they support. Avoid citation dumping, decorative theory, generic significance language, and literature summaries that never return to the research problem.

For humanities and interpretive social science, move carefully between close evidence, contextualization, concept development, and interpretive claim. For empirical social science, maintain alignment among research question, constructs, operationalization, sample, analysis, and conclusion.

Draft an abstract only from established information. Include, as appropriate: problem/context, gap or puzzle, question, materials/data, method, principal finding or argument, and contribution. Do not invent results for an unfinished study.

## Revision

Separate revision layers: problem and contribution; argument and section architecture; evidence and citation support; method and reporting transparency; paragraph coherence and style; and formatting and venue compliance.

When reviewer comments are supplied, build an action ledger:

| Comment | Underlying concern | Decision | Manuscript change | Response evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not silently change the author's theoretical commitment, interpretation, or empirical claim. Flag decisions that require author judgment.

## Quality check

Confirm that the conclusion answers the stated question, does not outrun the evidence, acknowledges limitations without empty ritual, and explains the contribution at the level actually demonstrated.

## Specialist revisions

Use `revision-continuity.md` for multi-round changes and cross-section consistency. Use `theory-contribution.md` for conceptual development and `qualitative-analysis.md` for interview-based interpretive claims. Treat researcher-proposed frameworks as analytical proposals unless their empirical origin and scope are established.
