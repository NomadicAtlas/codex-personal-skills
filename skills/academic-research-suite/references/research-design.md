# Research Design, Methods, and Reproducibility

Use this workflow for qualitative, quantitative, mixed-methods, historical, textual, computational, experimental, or human-subjects research design.

## Alignment chain

Maintain explicit alignment:

`research question → epistemic aim → unit of analysis → evidence/data → sampling/case logic → collection procedure → analytical procedure → warranted claim`

Diagnose breaks in this chain before adding methodological detail.

## Design choices

For every consequential choice, state the rationale, alternative considered, trade-off, and implication for inference.

- **Qualitative:** justify case and participant selection, reflexivity, access, saturation or information-power logic, coding/interpretive procedure, negative cases, and credibility practices. Do not impose pseudo-quantitative reliability measures when they conflict with the methodology's epistemology.
- **Humanities/history:** define corpus/archive construction, provenance, selection boundaries, contextual method, interpretive procedure, and handling of absence, mediation, and contested evidence.
- **Quantitative:** define constructs, measures, sampling frame, missing-data treatment, model assumptions, uncertainty, robustness checks, and the estimand or inferential target.
- **Mixed methods:** explain why integration is needed, where strands connect, and how divergence will be interpreted.
- **Experiments:** specify hypotheses, conditions, randomization, outcomes, exclusion rules, power or precision logic, analysis plan, and reproducibility artifacts.

## Human-subjects boundary

Identify privacy, consent, vulnerability, data protection, compensation, deception, and institutional-review issues. Provide planning support only. Do not present the output as legal advice, ethics approval, or an institutional determination.

## Analysis planning

Define what counts as evidence for each claim before analysis where possible. Separate confirmatory from exploratory work. For qualitative interpretation, document movement from material to codes/themes/concepts and preserve contradictory cases. For statistical analysis, report effect estimates and uncertainty, not significance alone.

## Reproducibility and reporting

Record data provenance, transformations, code or analytic decisions, versioned instruments, deviations from the plan, and access constraints. Never fabricate executed procedures, sample characteristics, reliability statistics, model outputs, or ethics approval identifiers.

## Deliverable

Provide a coherent design with assumptions, procedures, analysis plan, ethics and integrity safeguards, feasibility risks, and decisions still requiring the researcher's authority.

## Method-specific and execution boundaries

For qualitative interpretation, read `qualitative-analysis.md`; select quality criteria by methodological tradition rather than applying all credibility practices to every study. Distinguish what was performed, what is planned, and what is recommended. For established manuscripts, do not add data-collection or analytical procedures merely to match an ideal design.
