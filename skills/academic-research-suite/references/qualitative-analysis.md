# Qualitative analysis and interpretation

## Select the methodological logic

Recover the declared method and epistemology from the current manuscript and research decisions. Do not silently replace it. If unresolved, offer a provisional approach and ask only when the choice changes analysis. Verify method-specific advice against original methodological sources when needed; this guide is a routing aid, not a universal reporting standard.

- **IPA:** analyze each case in depth before cross-case comparison; preserve idiographic context, convergence and divergence, and the participant/researcher interpretive relationship. Do not turn a small purposive sample into prevalence estimates or require saturation as a default adequacy claim.
- **Reflexive thematic analysis:** make the researcher's interpretive role visible and explain theme construction. Do not automatically require independent coding, consensus coding, inter-rater reliability, or member checking. Distinguish this approach from codebook and coding-reliability variants.
- **Grounded theory:** identify the chosen tradition; explain constant comparison, memoing, category development, and theoretical sampling where actually used. Do not relabel a fixed interview dataset as theoretically sampled or claim theoretical saturation without support.
- **Discourse/narrative analysis:** identify the tradition and analytical object, attend to language, interaction, sequence, positioning, or narrative organization as appropriate. Do not reduce every approach to topical coding of reported attitudes.

Do not mix incompatible quality criteria. Justify sample adequacy through the study's aim, case depth, specificity, access, and chosen method; do not invent a completed procedure to satisfy a checklist.

## Preserve the inferential chain

Keep source locator and participant identifier, excerpt and context, participant meaning, researcher interpretation, within-case pattern, cross-case comparison, and theoretical claim distinct. Use a compact internal ledger when claims are complex; expose it only when useful or requested. Preserve anonymization and do not place identifying raw excerpts in external searches.

For each consequential theme, inspect supporting and divergent cases, evidence breadth, alternatives, and limits. Do not count quotations as independent participants or treat a memorable quotation as representative of all cases. Mark claims based only on a manuscript excerpt as provisional when transcripts have not been supplied.

## Audit frameworks and temporal claims

Distinguish sensitizing concepts used before analysis, concepts developed during analysis, and frameworks articulated during writing. Never invent their history. A framework proposed retrospectively is a candidate interpretation until supported and adopted. Do not describe dimensions as universal stages or a fixed sequence unless within-case temporal evidence supports that claim. Check whether recurrence, overlap, reversals, and nonparticipation challenge the proposed sequence.

## Report what happened

Separate completed collection/analysis, planned work, and recommended improvements. Do not insert photo elicitation, member checking, triangulation, second coding, or ethics procedures merely because they would strengthen a methods section. Preserve the distance between participant speech and interpretation, and label translated quotations and editorial omissions accurately.
