# Multi-round manuscript revision

## Establish the current source

Use the current identified manuscript and supplied instructions as authoritative. Record its filename/version, requested sections, revision goal, available supporting materials, and output form. Do not rely on an earlier summary when a newer manuscript exists. Read all relevant material before claiming a full-manuscript review; state any reading limits.

Recover settled research questions, methods, key terms, theoretical commitments, and earlier decisions. Treat them as working commitments, not immutable truths. Reopen one only when new evidence, a contradiction, or the user's instruction warrants it; explain the reason and consequence. Do not repeat intake already completed elsewhere.

## Make a bounded revision

Separate substantive analysis, argument structure, methods reporting, evidence/citations, prose, and formatting. Complete authorized changes directly. Mark recommendations requiring unavailable data rather than inventing completed work. Do not expand a local edit into a full rewrite without a task-based reason.

For substantial revisions, maintain a compact change ledger: location, original problem, action, evidence basis, and dependent sections. Keep it internal unless requested or useful for review. Preserve participant quotes and reference fields unless a requested correction specifically applies.

## Check propagation

Check changed concepts, sample descriptions, methods, findings, framework names, and claim strength across title, abstract, introduction, methods, findings, discussion, conclusion, tables, and figures as relevant. Reassess translations and citations affected by a substantive change. Do not claim changes in a response letter that were not made.

Summarize the actual scope, major analytical choices, verification performed, and material unresolved issues. Preserve a minimal continuity note with the manuscript when creating a handoff artifact; do not require the user to approve a recap before continuing authorized work.
