# Research Question and Contribution Scoping

Use this workflow when the topic is broad, the dissatisfaction is unclear, or the paper lacks an answerable research question.

## Intake

Recover or ask for the minimum information that changes the design:

1. What phenomenon, text, population, case, period, or corpus is under study?
2. What existing explanation, assumption, category, or empirical pattern feels inadequate?
3. What kind of answer is sought: interpretive, explanatory, comparative, descriptive, evaluative, causal, historical, or design-oriented?
4. What materials and access are realistically available?
5. What disciplinary audience or deliverable will judge success?

Do not ask questions already answered in the conversation or supplied files.

## Narrowing procedure

Translate the topic through this chain:

`topic → phenomenon → puzzle/tension → unit of analysis → context/boundary → analytical relation → answerable question`

Generate two or three candidate research questions when useful. For each, test specificity and answerability; theoretical significance; empirical or textual tractability; alignment between question, evidence, and method; contribution beyond applying a familiar theory to a new case; and feasibility within time, access, language, and ethics constraints.

## Contribution test

State contribution as a change in understanding:

- What does the literature currently assume or leave unresolved?
- What will this study show, distinguish, connect, reinterpret, or qualify?
- Why does that change matter for a scholarly conversation?

Avoid unsupported claims of being the first, only, or wholly original study. Prefer precise formulations such as refining a mechanism, revealing a boundary condition, reorganizing a debate, extending a concept, or supplying evidence that alters an interpretation.

## Deliverable

Produce a concise problem statement; one recommended research question plus viable alternatives; proposed analytical units and boundaries; a provisional contribution claim; evidence and method requirements; and unresolved decisions that require the user's judgment.
